Frequently asked questions

What is MAREA? An Android app for ocean anglers. It puts satellite sea temperature, chlorophyll, seabed depth, charted structure and marine forecasts on one map, and highlights water that matches what a target species is known to prefer.

Does MAREA show where the fish are? No. No app can see fish from space. MAREA shows where conditions match published preferences, and explains why in "Why here". Use it to decide where to look, together with your own knowledge and local reports.

What does the 0–100 habitat index mean? It shows how well the temperature, front, chlorophyll and structure at a spot match the species' preferences in MAREA's rules. It is not a probability of catching fish, and it has not been validated against catch data.

Which species are included? 36 species. Offshore: yellowfin, blackfin, albacore and Pacific bluefin tuna, mahi-mahi, wahoo, blue, black, striped and white marlin, sailfish, king mackerel and Atlantic Spanish mackerel. Snappers: Pacific red, spotted rose, yellow, Pacific dog, mutton, mangrove (gray), lane and Atlantic cubera snapper. Groupers: leopard, gulf, broomtail, Pacific goliath, Atlantic goliath, gag, red and black grouper. Inshore and others: roosterfish, white snook, common snook, Atlantic tarpon, crevalle jack, California yellowtail and longfin yellowtail. Each species is only suggested inside its known range. Several inshore species are Eastern Pacific only.

Where does the data come from? NOAA, NASA, the E.U. Copernicus Marine Service, OpenStreetMap, GEBCO, Florida FWC and SEMAR (Mexico). See the Data sources page. Every value in the app shows its source and time.

Why is part of the sea blank or hatched? Satellites cannot see through cloud, and some products do not cover every area. MAREA leaves gaps empty instead of guessing.

How current is the sea temperature? The top bar shows the observation time and age. GOES-19 updates hourly over the Americas. Global analyses update daily. Older data is always shown as older.

Does MAREA need internet? Yes, to load new data. Data you have already downloaded is kept on the phone and shown with its real age when you are offline.

How much mobile data does it use? Less than you might fear. On mobile data, MAREA uses NOAA's smaller ready-made sea-temperature maps, which are still live data. The large GOES-19 satellite download (about 70 MB at first, then hourly updates) waits for Wi-Fi. To allow it on mobile data, turn on Settings › Allow large downloads on mobile data (off by default). Android's data-usage screen shows exactly what MAREA has used.

Does MAREA track my location? No. Location is used only while the app is open, to show your position and distances. It is never sent to the developer. To fetch data, MAREA sends the area you are viewing (or your position, for "SST near me") to the data providers. See the privacy policy.

Is MAREA free? Are there ads? MAREA has no ads, no account and no in-app purchases. The price is shown on Google Play.

Can I use MAREA for navigation? No. MAREA is not a chart and not a safety tool. Use official nautical charts, marine forecasts and your vessel's instruments.

Does MAREA know fishing regulations or closed areas? It shows some charted restricted areas in U.S. waters, but it does not know fishing regulations, seasons, size limits or protected areas. Check your local authority.

Known limitations

  • Estimates, not observations of fish. The habitat index is not validated against catches.
  • Cloud blocks satellite temperature and chlorophyll. Coastal pixels are often missing.
  • Coverage varies: GOES-19 covers the Americas; charted structures and restricted areas are mainly U.S. waters; artificial reefs are Florida only; SEMAR tides cover some Oaxaca stations.
  • Current forecasts are model output and can be wrong, especially near the coast.
  • Depth is from a global relief model (cell sizes of hundreds of metres). It is not a sounding.
  • Large downloads and high memory use while building the GOES-19 composite. Low-memory phones may be slower.
  • Needs Android 7.0 or later on a 64-bit phone.
